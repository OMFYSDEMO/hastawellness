Can laser treat every pimple, scar or wart?

No. Active acne, acne marks, scars and warts are different concerns. Acne often needs medical skin care; laser or light treatment may be considered in selected cases. Some confirmed warts may be treated with laser, while other options may be more suitable. A clinician must assess the concern first.

What should I ask before a laser session?

Ask about the device, the practitioner’s experience with your skin tone, expected benefit, alternatives, discomfort, recovery and aftercare. Risks can include pigment changes, burns or scarring. Multiple visits may be needed; improvement and recurrence vary. No flawless-skin or scar-free result is guaranteed.

What if a spot changes or I’m unsure it is a wart?

Arrange medical assessment before cosmetic removal if a spot is new and unusual, changing, bleeding or not healing. A suspicious lesion may need specialist review or a biopsy rather than laser. Painful, deep or scarring acne also deserves timely clinical care.

WE CARE ABOUT THE PERSON, TOO

You deserve to feel heard.
Before you choose a treatment.

Unwanted hair can feel frustrating or affect how comfortable you feel day to day. We make room for that conversation, without judging your appearance or telling you how you should look. Tell the team what would help you feel comfortable during your visit.

New or increasing facial hair can sometimes be linked to hormones, including PCOS. Mention changes in your periods, acne or other symptoms during your consultation. A clinician may recommend further assessment; laser reduces hair and does not treat an underlying hormonal condition.

Will laser remove my hair permanently?

Laser aims for longer-term hair reduction. A series of sessions is usually needed, and maintenance may be needed, especially for facial hair influenced by hormones. Response varies with skin tone, hair type, treatment area and other factors.

Is it suitable for Indian skin tones and fine facial hair?

Suitability depends on the device, settings, skin tone and hair characteristics. Ask about the practitioner’s experience with your skin tone, the device used and whether a patch test is appropriate. Very fine, light, grey or white hair may respond poorly. Occasionally treatment can increase hair growth, particularly around the face and neck, so careful selection matters.

What should I understand before starting?

Your consultation should cover benefits, alternatives, preparation, eye protection and aftercare. Temporary redness and discomfort can occur; burns, pigment changes and scarring are possible. Tell the clinician about medicines, recent tanning and relevant health conditions. Ask about sun protection and planning treatments around important events.

These consultation-led programmes are for adults aged 18+. Areas, technology, availability, suitability and fees are confirmed by the clinic. If coarse facial hair appears suddenly with a deepening voice or other marked changes, seek prompt medical assessment.

Is PRP or GFC right for every type of hair loss?

No. Diagnosis comes first. PRP may be considered for selected hair-loss conditions. GFC protocols and supporting evidence vary; ask the clinician to explain the specific method and alternatives. Neither guarantees regrowth. Procedures can involve pain, bruising, bleeding or infection, and require a suitability assessment and consent.

Do I need a full vitamin panel or a stress test?

Not automatically. Tests depend on your history and examination. A wellbeing questionnaire can help guide a conversation, but does not diagnose a mental-health condition or measure stress through a single blood test. Persistent sleep difficulties or significant distress may need appropriate clinical referral.
